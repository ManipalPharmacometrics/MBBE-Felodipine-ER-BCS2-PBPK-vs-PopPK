Felodipine ER PopPK: model testing log

What was tried, what happened, and why the dual-Weibull model (M5) became the final PopPK model, with the absorption model M3 kept as the fallback.

Updated 25 Oct 2026Models tested 12 variants, 4 rounds of analysisArms 27 release formulations (T50 × Weibull shape)

Summary

The PopPK model has to reproduce the bioequivalence decision that the PBPK (PK-Sim) model gives across 27 release formulations, defined by the Weibull dissolution T50 and shape. Twelve structural variants were tested in three rounds.

  1. Disposition and absorption structure (M1 to M4, 16–23 Oct). A gut absorption step between release and plasma (M3) cut the T/R Cmax GMR error from up to 20 % (M1) to at most 3.5 %.
  2. Delay-style @delay input (M1d–M3d and no-lag variants, 24 Oct). With the release lag kept, @delay reproduces the ODE models exactly. Without the lag, fits and curves are much worse.
  3. Fixing M3's early peak (M3L, M3Lo, M5, M6, 24–25 Oct). A dual-Weibull release (M5) fits better on every arm and halves the Tmax bias. Estimating the lag or adding a transit chain does not help.

Final  M5: two Weibull release components in the @delay form, fixed 0.3113 h lag, first-order absorption, 2-compartment disposition, fitted per arm.

Fallback  M3: one Weibull release, first-order absorption, 2-compartment disposition.

Round 1 · 16–23 Oct 2026

Disposition and absorption structure

M1: 1-compartment with Weibull release
The first production model, fitted on all 27 arms. It had two likelihood basins. In 23 of 27 arms the best fit was a fast "release" (implied T50 0.3–2.4 h) with a very large Vc of about 4000 L, so release and distribution were not separated. Cmax/PBPK ranged 0.87–1.135 across arms, which biased the T/R Cmax GMR by up to 20 %, and the PopPK safe space drifted away from PBPK off the reference shape.
M2: 2-compartment with Weibull release
Q or Vp ran to their bounds from either start. The peripheral compartment is not identified without an absorption step. Rejected.
M3: Weibull release, first-order absorption, 2-compartment
The gut compartment separates release from appearance in plasma, which gives PBPK's fast rise and plateau without forcing release to be fast. On all 27 arms its AIC beat M1 on 27/27, Cmax/PBPK was 0.976–1.043 and the GMR error at most 3.5 %. Remaining problems: the fitted T50 is about twice PK-Sim's (about 9.4 h at the reference 4.78 h), Vc reaches about 2500 L at shape 1.5, and the curve peaks 0.4–0.7 h early at shape 0.764 and above.
M4: M3 paired, reference and test in the same subjects
Fitted to two arms at once with shared disposition and separate release per period. It was much worse than two separate M3 fits: several fits produced non-finite predictions for up to 75 of 100 subjects, and the GMR error was −12 % to +15 %. In the PBPK model the release rate changes where along the gut the drug is absorbed, and so how much gut-wall CYP3A4 extraction it sees. Disposition as seen from the gut is therefore not shared across formulations. Rejected.
Round 2 · 24 Oct 2026

Delay-style @delay input

Question. Do the results stay the same if the hand-written Weibull hazard ODE is replaced by the Pumas built-in delay form, @delay(Weibull(shape, scale), cmt) with the dose at bioavailability 0?

  • The first version had no lag, as in the plain @delay form. Fits were much worse (M3d0 reference LL −755 against −354), M1 and M2 peaked too early, and M3d0 overshot by 60 % at 0.5 h.
  • Pumas ignores @dosecontrol lags when @delay is used: the output is identical with and without them.
  • Tlag + Weibull gives an infinite likelihood for shape below 1, and so does a dose record placed at t = lag.
  • The working form is @delay(Tlag + truncated(Weibull(γ, α), 1e-4, Inf), cmt). The truncation removes at most 0.4 % of the release (at shape 0.5), less than the 0.01 h clock floor M3 already used.
  • With the lag, M1d, M2d and M3d reproduce M1, M2 and M3: log-likelihoods within 3 units, identical Tmax and mean curves, the same GMRs, and similar run times.

Conclusion. The @delay form is equivalent and was adopted for the new structures. The lag must stay.

Round 3 · 24–25 Oct 2026

Fixing M3's early peak

M3 rises correctly but peaks early and falls too fast, worst at shape 1.5 (Tmax 5.3 h against 6.0 h, with only 28 % of subjects within 0.5 h of PK-Sim). Two physiological explanations were checked first.

  • Regional gut-wall extraction varying with release rate: rejected. AUC per fraction absorbed is flat within ±3 % across the 27 arms.
  • Deconvolution against PK-Sim's "absorbed into mucosa" output: inconclusive, with implied half-lives of 2 h or less.

That leaves a structural cause: one release timescale and one ka cannot give both the fast initial phase and the long late tail. Four variants were tested on the diagnostic arms.

Caveats

  • The two release components describe the curve; they are not two physical processes. The fast fraction ranges 0.02–0.97 across arms, and on some arms the first component is a 0.2–0.4 h burst.
  • The mixture's 50 % release time is about 8–9 h where PK-Sim uses 4–5 h, the same offset as M3, because the PopPK input lumps release, gut transit and absorption.
  • At the reference arm, the fast fraction (RSE 45 %) and Q and Vp (RSE 55–70 %) are poorly determined. Vc reaches about 2300 L at shape 1.5, as in M3.
  • One arm, 6.31 h / 1.2, still peaks 0.85 h early.
  • The 2-D safe-space limits sit slightly low because M5 overpredicts the reference-arm Cmax by 0.9 %, so its T/R GMRs run about 1.5 % low at shape 0.9 and above.
